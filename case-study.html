PillBox — Medication Tracker App

Helping patients with chronic conditions stay on top of their meds without the anxiety of double-dosing.

Product Designer, UX Researcher · 8 weeks · iOS & Android

the problem.

Medication non-adherence affects over 50% of patients with chronic conditions. People forget doses, mix up prescriptions, or stop taking meds when side effects kick in — and the consequences range from prolonged illness to preventable hospital visits. Most existing apps are either too clinical, too complex, or built for caregivers rather than the patients themselves.

Patients don’t need another complex health tool. They need something that feels less like a chore and more like a companion.

research & discovery.

I started with a mixed-methods approach: 12 user interviews with patients managing 2+ prescriptions, a competitive audit of 6 medication apps, and a survey with 50+ respondents. Three patterns stood out immediately.

1

Forgetting isn’t the root cause

Patients remembered to take their meds — they forgot whether they had already taken them. Double-dosing anxiety was the top concern across every interview.

2

Pill fatigue is real

Users with 5+ daily prescriptions described feeling overwhelmed by the sheer volume. Visual pill identification and schedule chunking were consistently requested.

3

Connection to providers is broken

When side effects occurred, patients wanted to reach their doctor — but the path was always indirect. A built-in feedback loop was a clear opportunity.

design process.

With research insights in hand, I moved into wireframing and iterative prototyping. The core challenge was balancing simplicity with the depth needed for medication management. I explored three directions before converging on a card-based dashboard that surfaces the most time-sensitive action first.

Early wireframes and low-fidelity prototypes used in the first round of usability testing.

the solution.

The final design centers on three core screens: a Today view that shows only what’s relevant right now, a Pill identity card with visual recognition so patients can confirm they’re taking the right medication, and a Provider connect feature that lets patients report side effects directly from the app.

build & handoff.

Designed entirely in Figma using auto-layout, components, and variants for a clean developer handoff. The design system includes 30+ reusable components, a defined spacing and color token system, and interaction specs for every state — default, pressed, loading, empty, and error.
